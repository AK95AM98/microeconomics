# Concept Taxonomy

This taxonomy groups the 242 concepts in the ECON 1101 learning graph into
**16 categories** — one per existing textbook chapter — rather than the
~12-category target the learning-graph-generator skill suggests by default.

This is an intentional deviation: the course description's topic outline
(`docs/course-description.md`) already organizes the material into 16
chapters across 6 units, and this book's 16 chapters already contain full,
real content (see [Course Description Assessment](course-description-assessment.md),
Section 7). Mapping one taxonomy category to each chapter keeps the learning
graph traceable 1:1 against the existing book structure, which matters more
here than hitting a generic category-count target — and every category still
comfortably clears the balance requirement (largest category is 9.9% of all
concepts, well under the 30% ceiling; see
[Taxonomy Distribution Report](taxonomy-distribution.md)).

| Category | TaxonomyID | Count | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foundations | FOUND | 17 | Scarcity, opportunity cost, marginal analysis, the production frontier, comparative advantage, and the positive/normative distinction — the toolkit every later chapter draws on. |
| Supply and Demand | SUPDEM | 24 | Markets, the laws of demand and supply, market equilibrium, and consumer/producer surplus. |
| Elasticity | ELAS | 14 | Price, income, and cross-price elasticity of demand and supply, and their policy applications (tax incidence). |
| Consumer Theory | CONS | 14 | Utility, indifference curves, budget constraints, and the consumer optimum underlying the demand curve. |
| Production and Costs | PROD | 18 | Production functions, product and cost curves, and economies of scale underlying firm supply decisions. |
| Perfect Competition | PCOMP | 11 | Price-taking behavior, the MR = MC rule, the shutdown decision, and long-run allocative efficiency. |
| Monopoly | MONOP | 16 | Market power, monopoly pricing, deadweight loss, price discrimination, and antitrust policy. |
| Monopolistic Competition | MCOMP | 11 | Product differentiation, short- and long-run equilibrium, and excess capacity. |
| Oligopoly | OLIG | 13 | Strategic interdependence, the Cournot and Bertrand models, collusion, and market-concentration measures. |
| Game Theory | GAME | 12 | Payoff matrices, dominant strategies, Nash equilibrium, and repeated/sequential games. |
| Factor Markets | FACTOR | 17 | Derived demand for labor, wage determination, monopsony, capital markets, and economic rent. |
| Market Failures | MKTFAIL | 16 | The general taxonomy of market failure: externalities, public goods, information asymmetry, and government failure. |
| Externalities and Public Goods | EXTPUB | 14 | The deep-dive on correcting externalities (Pigouvian taxes/subsidies, the Coase theorem, cap-and-trade) and providing public goods (the Samuelson condition, the free-rider problem). |
| Information Economics | INFO | 10 | The deep-dive on asymmetric information: the market for lemons, signaling, screening, and principal-agent problems. |
| Behavioral Economics | BEHAV | 16 | Bounded rationality, prospect theory, present bias, framing effects, and nudges. |
| International Trade | TRADE | 19 | Comparative-advantage trade models, factor endowments, tariffs and quotas, and the balance of payments. |

**Note on sequencing:** Market Failures (MKTFAIL) deliberately holds the
general/introductory version of several concepts (Externality, Adverse
Selection, Moral Hazard, Pigouvian Tax) that Externalities and Public Goods
(EXTPUB) and Information Economics (INFO) then build on in depth — this
mirrors the existing chapters, where Chapter 12 introduces these ideas and
Chapters 13–14 elaborate them.
