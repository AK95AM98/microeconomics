# References: Consumer Theory

1. [Utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility) - Wikipedia - Covers the concept of utility as a measure of consumer satisfaction, including total and marginal utility, the foundation of this chapter's opening sections.

2. [Indifference curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indifference_curve) - Wikipedia - Explains how indifference curves represent bundles yielding equal satisfaction and how the marginal rate of substitution is read from their slope, matching the chapter's graphical consumer theory.

3. [Budget constraint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_constraint) - Wikipedia - Describes how income and prices define the set of affordable bundles, directly supporting the chapter's budget-line construction and consumer-optimum section.

4. Mathematical Psychics - Francis Ysidro Edgeworth - Kegan Paul - Edgeworth invented the indifference curve in 1881 as a tool for comparing consumption bundles, the graphical device this chapter uses to derive the consumer optimum.

5. Intermediate Microeconomics: A Modern Approach (9th ed.) - Hal R. Varian - W. W. Norton - Varian's text gives the now-standard rigorous treatment of the tangency condition between indifference curves and the budget line that this chapter relies on to find the utility-maximizing bundle.

6. [Marginal utility and the law of diminishing marginal utility](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/choices-opportunity-cost-tutorial) - Khan Academy - Covers total and marginal utility with worked schedules similar to the chapter's pizza-slice example.

7. [Indifference Curve](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/i/indifferencecurve.asp) - Investopedia - Defines indifference curves and their properties (downward-sloping, convex, non-intersecting), reinforcing the chapter's graphical analysis.

8. [Marginal utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_utility) - Wikipedia - Explains diminishing marginal utility, the principle underlying the chapter's equal marginal principle and utility-maximization derivation.

9. [14.01 Principles of Microeconomics, Consumer Theory lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01sc-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2011/pages/lecture-notes/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - Lecture notes covering utility maximization, indifference curves, and income/substitution effects at the same depth as this chapter.

10. [Consumer choice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_choice) - Wikipedia - Surveys the broader theory of consumer choice underlying utility maximization, complementing the chapter's budget-constraint and tangency-condition derivation.
