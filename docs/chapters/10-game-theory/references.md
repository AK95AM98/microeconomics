# References: Game Theory

1. [Nash equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nash_equilibrium) - Wikipedia - Defines the equilibrium concept where no player can benefit by unilaterally changing strategy, the central solution concept this chapter teaches.

2. [Prisoner's dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner%27s_dilemma) - Wikipedia - Explains the classic game where individually dominant strategies lead to a mutually worse outcome, matching the chapter's core worked example.

3. [Game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory) - Wikipedia - Surveys the broader field of strategic decision-making, including payoff matrices, dominant strategies, and sequential games, framing the chapter as a whole.

4. "Non-Cooperative Games" - John F. Nash Jr. - Annals of Mathematics (1951) - Nash's paper introduced the equilibrium concept bearing his name, the tool this chapter uses to solve every simultaneous game after the prisoner's dilemma.

5. The Evolution of Cooperation - Robert Axelrod - Basic Books (1984) - Axelrod's repeated tournament experiments showed that tit-for-tat sustains cooperation in repeated games, underlying the chapter's section on how repeated interaction can support cooperative outcomes.

6. [Game theory of oligopoly behavior](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/monopolistic-competition-oligopoly-tutorial/game-theory-tutorial) - Khan Academy - Introduces payoff matrices, dominant strategies, and Nash equilibrium through oligopoly examples, matching the chapter's applications section.

7. [Nash Equilibrium](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/n/nash-equilibrium.asp) - Investopedia - Defines Nash equilibrium with simple business examples, reinforcing the chapter's formal definition and worked payoff matrices.

8. [Backward induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backward_induction) - Wikipedia - Explains how backward induction solves sequential games, directly supporting the chapter's section on sequential games and non-credible threats.

9. [14.01 Principles of Microeconomics, Game Theory lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01sc-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2011/pages/lecture-notes/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - Lecture notes covering simultaneous and sequential games, backward induction, and Nash equilibrium at the same level as this chapter.

10. [Game Theory](https://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/GameTheory.html) - Econlib (Library of Economics and Liberty) - An encyclopedia entry surveying game theory's core concepts and applications to economics, supporting the chapter's broader framing.
