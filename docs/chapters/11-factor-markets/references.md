# References: Factor Markets

1. [Labor economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_economics) - Wikipedia - Surveys labor supply, labor demand, and wage determination, the broad field this chapter's competitive-labor-market section draws from.

2. [Monopsony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopsony) - Wikipedia - Explains how a single buyer of labor depresses wages and employment below the competitive level, directly matching the chapter's monopsony section.

3. [Economic rent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_rent) - Wikipedia - Defines economic rent as payment to a factor beyond what is needed to keep it in its current use, matching the chapter's closing distinction between rent and other factor payments.

4. The Distribution of Wealth - John Bates Clark - Macmillan (1899) - Clark developed the marginal productivity theory of distribution, the foundation for the marginal revenue product concept this chapter uses to determine a firm's profit-maximizing hiring level.

5. Labor Economics (8th ed.) - George J. Borjas - McGraw-Hill - Borjas's textbook provides the standard modern treatment of the labor supply work-leisure trade-off and competitive wage determination that this chapter's equilibrium-wage section follows.

6. [Factor markets: labor demand and supply](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/factor-markets) - Khan Academy - Covers marginal revenue product, labor demand, and labor supply, closely matching this chapter's hiring-decision derivation.

7. [Marginal Revenue Product](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/marginal-revenue-product-mrp.asp) - Investopedia - Defines MRP and its use in determining how many workers a firm should hire, reinforcing the chapter's worked bakery example.

8. [Human capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital) - Wikipedia - Explains education and training as an investment in future earnings, supporting the chapter's human-capital-theory section.

9. [Derived Demand](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/d/derived-demand.asp) - Investopedia - Explains how demand for an input is derived from demand for the output it produces, matching the chapter's opening section on input markets.

10. [The Economy 1.0, Unit 6: The firm and its employees](https://www.core-econ.org/the-economy/book/text/06.html) - CORE Econ - Discusses wage determination and the employment relationship, supporting the chapter's broader treatment of how factor prices are set.
