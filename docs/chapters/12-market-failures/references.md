# References: Market Failures

1. [Market failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_failure) - Wikipedia - Surveys the general conditions under which markets fail to allocate resources efficiently, matching the chapter's overview taxonomy of market failure categories.

2. [Externality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externality) - Wikipedia - Explains how costs or benefits can spill onto third parties, matching the chapter's negative- and positive-externality section.

3. [Information asymmetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_asymmetry) - Wikipedia - Covers how unequal information between buyers and sellers can cause markets to fail, matching the chapter's adverse-selection and moral-hazard overview.

4. "The Anatomy of Market Failure" - Francis M. Bator - Quarterly Journal of Economics (1958) - Bator's article coined the term "market failure" and built the taxonomy (externalities, public goods, information problems, market power) that structures this entire chapter.

5. "The Pure Theory of Public Expenditure" - Paul A. Samuelson - Review of Economics and Statistics (1954) - Samuelson's efficiency condition for public-goods provision underlies the chapter's brief treatment of non-excludable, non-rival goods and the free-rider problem.

6. [Market failure and the role of government](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/market-failure-tutorial) - Khan Academy - A unit covering externalities, public goods, and government responses to market failure, matching this chapter's survey structure.

7. [Market Failure](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/marketfailure.asp) - Investopedia - Defines market failure and lists its main causes with real-world examples, reinforcing the chapter's checklist-style approach.

8. [Public good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_good_%28economics%29) - Wikipedia - Explains non-excludability and non-rivalry, the defining properties of public goods covered in the chapter's market-failure taxonomy.

9. [Asymmetric Information](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/asymmetricinformation.asp) - Investopedia - Explains how one-sided information can prevent efficient trades, supporting the chapter's preview of adverse selection and moral hazard (expanded in Chapter 14).

10. [Rent-seeking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rent-seeking) - Wikipedia - Explains how individuals and firms expend resources lobbying for favorable regulation rather than creating value, illustrating the chapter's closing discussion of government failure.
