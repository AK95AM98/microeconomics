# References: Externalities and Public Goods

1. [Pigovian tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pigovian_tax) - Wikipedia - Explains how a tax set equal to the marginal external cost can restore the efficient quantity, matching the chapter's optimal-Pigouvian-tax calculation.

2. [Coase theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coase_theorem) - Wikipedia - Covers the proposition that private bargaining can achieve an efficient outcome when transaction costs are low, directly matching the chapter's Coase-theorem section.

3. [Free rider problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_rider_problem) - Wikipedia - Explains why individuals underprovide a non-excludable public good, matching the chapter's game-theoretic free-rider example.

4. The Economics of Welfare (4th ed.) - Arthur C. Pigou - Macmillan (1920) - Pigou proposed taxing activities that generate negative externalities by the size of the external cost, the "Pigouvian tax" this chapter calculates for the steel-mill pollution example.

5. "The Problem of Social Cost" - Ronald H. Coase - Journal of Law and Economics (1960) - Coase showed that, absent transaction costs, private parties can bargain to the efficient outcome regardless of how property rights are initially assigned, the theorem this chapter works through with a bargaining example.

6. [Externalities and public goods](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/market-failure-tutorial/externalities-tutorial) - Khan Academy - Covers negative and positive externalities, Pigouvian taxes and subsidies, and public-goods provision, closely matching this chapter's structure.

7. [Pigouvian Tax](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/pigovian-tax.asp) - Investopedia - Defines a Pigouvian tax and gives real-world examples like carbon taxes, reinforcing the chapter's policy-tool discussion.

8. [Emissions trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emissions_trading) - Wikipedia - Explains how cap-and-trade systems achieve pollution reduction at least cost, directly supporting the chapter's cap-and-trade section.

9. [Tragedy of the commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tragedy_of_the_commons) - Wikipedia - Explains why shared, non-excludable resources tend to be overused absent property rights or regulation, complementing the chapter's common-pool-resources section.

10. [Congestion pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestion_pricing) - Wikipedia - Explains how usage-based pricing manages a congestible common-pool resource, directly supporting the chapter's closing section on congestion pricing.
