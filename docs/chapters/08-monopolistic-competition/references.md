# References: Monopolistic Competition

1. [Monopolistic competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopolistic_competition) - Wikipedia - Defines the market structure that blends many sellers with product differentiation, matching the chapter's opening comparison table of market structures.

2. [Product differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_differentiation) - Wikipedia - Explains how firms distinguish their products to gain limited pricing power, directly supporting the chapter's section on differentiation and brand loyalty.

3. [Imperfect competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperfect_competition) - Wikipedia - Covers the broader category of markets between perfect competition and monopoly, framing the chapter's discussion of downward-sloping but highly elastic firm demand.

4. The Theory of Monopolistic Competition - Edward H. Chamberlin - Harvard University Press (1933) - Chamberlin originated the monopolistic competition model itself, including the long-run "excess capacity" theorem this chapter uses to explain why firms produce below minimum efficient scale.

5. Microeconomics (9th ed.) - Robert S. Pindyck and Daniel L. Rubinfeld - Pearson - Pindyck and Rubinfeld provide the calculus-based short-run and long-run profit-maximization graphs for a differentiated firm that this chapter's price-and-output derivation follows.

6. [Monopolistic competition and the long-run equilibrium](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/monopolistic-competition-oligopoly-tutorial) - Khan Academy - Walks through short-run profit, long-run entry, and the resulting zero-economic-profit outcome for a monopolistically competitive firm, mirroring this chapter's graphs.

7. [Monopolistic Competition](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/monopolisticmarket.asp) - Investopedia - Explains how differentiated products let firms set prices above marginal cost without eliminating competition, reinforcing the chapter's pricing-power discussion.

8. [Advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising) - Wikipedia - Surveys the economics of advertising, supporting the chapter's discussion of informative versus persuasive advertising as non-price competition.

9. [The Economy 1.0, Unit 7: The firm and its customers](https://www.core-econ.org/the-economy/book/text/07.html) - CORE Econ - Discusses how firms compete on price and quality when products are not identical, supporting the chapter's efficiency-versus-variety trade-off discussion.

10. [Edward Chamberlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_H._Chamberlin) - Wikipedia - Biography of the economist who originated the monopolistic-competition model and its excess-capacity theorem, giving students the history behind the chapter's central framework.
