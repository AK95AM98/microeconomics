# References: Behavioral Economics

1. [Prospect theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_theory) - Wikipedia - Explains how people evaluate gains and losses relative to a reference point rather than final wealth, the chapter's central alternative to expected-utility theory.

2. [Loss aversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_aversion) - Wikipedia - Covers the finding that losses loom larger than equivalent gains, directly matching the chapter's prospect-theory section.

3. [Hyperbolic discounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_discounting) - Wikipedia - Explains present bias and time-inconsistent preferences, matching the chapter's section contrasting hyperbolic with standard exponential discounting.

4. "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk" - Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky - Econometrica (1979) - Kahneman and Tversky's paper introduced prospect theory, including loss aversion and reference dependence, the framework this chapter contrasts with standard expected-utility theory.

5. Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness - Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein - Yale University Press (2008) - Thaler and Sunstein developed "libertarian paternalism" and the nudge concept this chapter applies to real policy design, such as retirement-savings defaults.

6. [Behavioral economics: biases and nudges](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/choices-opportunity-cost-tutorial/behavioral-economics) - Khan Academy - Introduces bounded rationality, cognitive biases, and nudges, matching this chapter's survey of heuristics and biases.

7. [Loss Aversion](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/l/loss-psychology.asp) - Investopedia - Explains loss aversion with investing examples, reinforcing the chapter's prospect-theory discussion.

8. [Nudge theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nudge_theory) - Wikipedia - Surveys nudge-based policy design and libertarian paternalism, directly supporting the chapter's closing section.

9. [Present Bias](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/present-bias.asp) - Investopedia - Defines present bias and hyperbolic discounting with everyday examples like procrastination and saving, matching the chapter's time-preference section.

10. [Behavioral Economics](https://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/BehavioralEconomics.html) - Econlib (Library of Economics and Liberty) - An encyclopedia entry surveying how psychology has reshaped standard economic models, supporting the chapter's broader framing of bounded rationality.
