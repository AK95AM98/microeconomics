# References: Perfect Competition

1. [Perfect competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_competition) - Wikipedia - Lays out the four defining conditions of perfectly competitive markets, matching the chapter's opening characterization section.

2. [Marginal revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_revenue) - Wikipedia - Explains why marginal revenue equals price for a price-taking firm, the key simplification this chapter uses to derive the MR = MC rule.

3. [Economic profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_profit) - Wikipedia - Distinguishes accounting profit from economic profit, needed to understand why free entry drives economic profit to zero in the chapter's long-run equilibrium section.

4. Microeconomics (9th ed.) - Robert S. Pindyck and Daniel L. Rubinfeld - Pearson - Pindyck and Rubinfeld give the calculus-based derivation of the MR = MC profit-maximization rule for a price-taking firm that this chapter's shutdown and supply analysis builds on.

5. Risk, Uncertainty, and Profit - Frank H. Knight - Houghton Mifflin (1921) - Knight's analysis of competitive markets under free entry provided the classic rationale for why economic profit is driven to zero in long-run competitive equilibrium, the chapter's central long-run result.

6. [Perfect competition and the firm's shutdown decision](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/firm-economic-profit-tutorial/perfect-competition-topic) - Khan Academy - Covers the price-taking firm, the MR = MC rule, and the short-run shutdown decision, matching this chapter's core derivations step for step.

7. [Perfect Competition](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/perfectcompetition.asp) - Investopedia - Defines the characteristics of a perfectly competitive market and contrasts it with other market structures, reinforcing the chapter's benchmark framing.

8. [Marginal cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_cost) - Wikipedia - Explains how marginal cost is defined and computed, directly supporting the chapter's MR = MC profit-maximization rule.

9. [14.01 Principles of Microeconomics, Perfectly Competitive Markets lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01sc-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2011/pages/lecture-notes/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - Lecture notes on short-run and long-run competitive equilibrium that parallel this chapter's treatment of zero long-run profit.

10. [Allocative efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allocative_efficiency) - Wikipedia - Explains the efficiency condition achieved at competitive equilibrium, directly supporting the chapter's conclusion that perfect competition maximizes total surplus.
