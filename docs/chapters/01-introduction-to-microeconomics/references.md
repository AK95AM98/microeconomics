# References: Introduction to Microeconomics

1. [Opportunity cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_cost) - Wikipedia - Explains the core idea that the true cost of any choice is the value of the next-best alternative foregone, the organizing concept for this chapter's scarcity and trade-off discussion.

2. [Production–possibility frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production%E2%80%93possibility_frontier) - Wikipedia - Covers the PPF curve, its bowed-out shape, and how points on, inside, and outside it represent efficiency, directly matching the chapter's PPF construction section.

3. [Comparative advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_advantage) - Wikipedia - Introduces the principle that specialization and trade benefit from differing opportunity costs rather than absolute productivity, previewing the chapter's trade discussion.

4. Economics (18th ed.) - Paul A. Samuelson - McGraw-Hill - Samuelson popularized the Production Possibilities Frontier as a teaching diagram in his landmark 1948 textbook, the direct ancestor of the PPF graphic this chapter builds.

5. Principles of Economics (9th ed.) - N. Gregory Mankiw - Cengage Learning - Mankiw's "Ten Principles of Economics" framing, especially "people face trade-offs" and "the cost of something is what you give up," structures this chapter's opening treatment of scarcity and opportunity cost.

6. [Scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/scarcity-opportunity-cost-tutorial) - Khan Academy - A free unit of videos and practice problems on scarcity, opportunity cost, and the PPF that mirrors this chapter's core learning objectives for self-paced review.

7. [Economic efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_efficiency) - Wikipedia - Surveys the broader concept of efficiency that the PPF illustrates graphically, reinforcing why points on the frontier represent efficient production.

8. [Opportunity Cost](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/o/opportunitycost.asp) - Investopedia - A plain-language definition and worked examples of opportunity cost in everyday financial decisions, useful for students connecting the textbook concept to real choices.

9. [14.01 Principles of Microeconomics](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01sc-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2011/) - MIT OpenCourseWare - The full syllabus and lecture notes from MIT's introductory microeconomics course, whose opening lectures on scarcity and marginal analysis parallel this chapter.

10. [Scarcity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarcity) - Wikipedia - Defines scarcity as the basic economic problem of limited resources relative to unlimited wants, the foundational idea this chapter opens with.
