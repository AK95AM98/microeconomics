# References: Monopoly

1. [Monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly) - Wikipedia - Surveys the sources of monopoly power and barriers to entry, matching the chapter's opening section on why monopolies persist.

2. [Price discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_discrimination) - Wikipedia - Explains first-, second-, and third-degree price discrimination and their welfare effects, directly matching the chapter's price-discrimination section.

3. [United States antitrust law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_antitrust_law) - Wikipedia - Covers the legal framework (Sherman Act, Clayton Act) used to curb monopoly power, supporting the chapter's antitrust-policy discussion.

4. The Economics of Welfare (4th ed.) - Arthur C. Pigou - Macmillan (1920) - Pigou coined the terms "first-degree," "second-degree," and "third-degree" price discrimination still used in this chapter to classify how monopolists capture consumer surplus.

5. The Economics of Imperfect Competition - Joan Robinson - Macmillan (1933) - Robinson's formal analysis of a single seller facing a downward-sloping demand curve underlies this chapter's derivation of monopoly marginal revenue and the resulting deadweight loss.

6. [Monopoly: price and output determination](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/monopoly-tutorial) - Khan Academy - Derives a monopolist's marginal revenue curve and profit-maximizing price and quantity, matching this chapter's core graphical derivation.

7. [Monopoly](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/m/monopoly.asp) - Investopedia - Explains how a single seller with no close substitutes sets price above marginal cost, reinforcing the chapter's comparison with perfect competition.

8. [Deadweight loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadweight_loss) - Wikipedia - Explains how market power creates deadweight loss relative to the competitive outcome, supporting the chapter's monopoly-inefficiency analysis.

9. [Natural monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_monopoly) - Wikipedia - Explains why large fixed costs and economies of scale can make a single firm the efficient market outcome, supporting the chapter's natural-monopoly and regulation section.

10. [Antitrust Laws](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/antitrust-laws.asp) - Investopedia - Summarizes the major U.S. antitrust statutes and landmark cases, reinforcing the chapter's discussion of how regulators respond to monopoly power.
