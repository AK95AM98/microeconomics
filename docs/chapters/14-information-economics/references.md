# References: Information Economics

1. [The Market for Lemons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Market_for_Lemons) - Wikipedia - Covers Akerlof's model of a used-car market where sellers know more than buyers, the chapter's founding example of adverse selection.

2. [Adverse selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_selection) - Wikipedia - Explains how hidden characteristics known before a contract is signed can cause high-risk participants to dominate a market, matching the chapter's adverse-selection section.

3. [Moral hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moral_hazard) - Wikipedia - Covers how hidden actions taken after a contract is signed change behavior, directly matching the chapter's distinction between adverse selection and moral hazard.

4. "The Market for 'Lemons': Quality Uncertainty and the Market Mechanism" - George A. Akerlof - Quarterly Journal of Economics (1970) - Akerlof's paper founded the economics of adverse selection, the used-car "lemons" model this chapter's price-quality spiral example is built directly from.

5. "Job Market Signaling" - A. Michael Spence - Quarterly Journal of Economics (1973) - Spence showed how an informed party (e.g., a job applicant) can credibly reveal private information through a costly signal like education, the basis for the chapter's signaling section.

6. [Asymmetric information: adverse selection and moral hazard](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics/market-failure-tutorial/asymmetric-information-tutorial) - Khan Academy - Covers adverse selection, moral hazard, and the lemons problem with examples from insurance and used cars, matching this chapter's applications section.

7. [Adverse Selection](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/a/adverseselection.asp) - Investopedia - Defines adverse selection with insurance-market examples, reinforcing the chapter's health-insurance application.

8. [Signalling (economics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signalling_%28economics%29) - Wikipedia - Contrasts signaling by informed parties with screening by uninformed parties, directly supporting the chapter's section on coping with asymmetric information.

9. [Principal-Agent Problem](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/principal-agent-problem.asp) - Investopedia - Explains how misaligned incentives between a principal and an agent arise and how incentive contracts address them, matching the chapter's principal-agent section.

10. [Principal–agent problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal%E2%80%93agent_problem) - Wikipedia - Covers the broader principal-agent framework underlying moral hazard, connecting the chapter's insurance and labor-contract examples to the wider literature on misaligned incentives.
