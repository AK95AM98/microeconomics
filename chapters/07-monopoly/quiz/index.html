    Quiz: Monopoly

    Test your understanding of monopoly pricing, deadweight loss, price discrimination, and natural monopoly regulation with these review questions.

    1. What defines a "monopoly"?

    1. A market with many sellers producing slightly differentiated products
    2. A market with a single seller of a good that has no close substitutes
    3. A market with a few large firms whose decisions depend on each other
    4. A market where firms are price takers facing perfectly elastic demand
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. A monopoly exists when a single firm is the only seller of a good with no close substitutes, giving it substantial control over price. Option A describes monopolistic competition, option C describes oligopoly, and option D describes the price-taking behavior of a perfectly competitive firm, the opposite of monopoly power.

    Concept Tested: Monopoly

    2. Why is a monopolist called a "price maker"?

    1. Because it must accept whatever price the market determines
    2. Because it faces a horizontal demand curve at the market price
    3. Because it chooses a point on the downward-sloping market demand curve rather than accepting a price set externally
    4. Because government regulation sets its price directly
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Because the monopolist is the only seller, the market demand curve is its own demand curve, so it selects the price-quantity combination on that curve rather than accepting a price determined independently. Options A and B describe a price-taking competitive firm instead, and option D wrongly assumes all monopolies are regulated, when many are not.

    Concept Tested: Price Maker

    3. What causes a "natural monopoly" to arise?

    1. A patent that grants exclusive legal rights to sell a product
    2. A government franchise designating a single provider
    3. Network effects that make a product more valuable as more people use it
    4. Average total cost declining over the entire relevant range of market demand, so one firm can supply the market more cheaply than several smaller firms
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A natural monopoly arises specifically from large fixed costs and economies of scale that cause average total cost to fall throughout the relevant range of demand, as with electricity transmission, making a single large firm more efficient than multiple smaller ones. Options A, B, and C describe other distinct barriers to entry — legal restrictions, government franchise, and network effects — that can sustain monopoly power without being the specific cost-based cause of a natural monopoly.

    Concept Tested: Natural Monopoly

    4. Why does a monopolist's marginal revenue curve lie below its demand curve at every positive quantity?

    1. Because the monopolist must lower the price on all units sold, not just the marginal unit, to sell more
    2. Because marginal cost always exceeds marginal revenue for a monopolist
    3. Because the monopolist faces a perfectly elastic demand curve
    4. Because total revenue always falls as quantity increases
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Since the monopolist faces the entire downward-sloping market demand curve, selling one more unit requires cutting the price on every unit sold, not just the additional one, so marginal revenue falls faster than price. Option B incorrectly states a cost relationship that isn't generally true, option C describes a price taker rather than a monopolist, and option D is false since total revenue rises (while marginal revenue is still positive) before eventually falling.

    Concept Tested: Monopolist Marginal Revenue

    5. Why does unregulated monopoly create a deadweight loss relative to a perfectly competitive outcome in the same market?

    1. Because the monopolist produces more output than the competitive quantity
    2. Because consumer surplus always falls to zero under monopoly
    3. Because producer surplus always falls under monopoly
    4. Because the monopolist restricts output below the competitive quantity, leaving some mutually beneficial trades between willing buyers and the firm's marginal cost unexploited
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. A monopolist sets \(MR=MC\) and reads price off the demand curve, producing less than the competitive quantity where \(P=MC\); the surplus from the forgone units between the monopoly and competitive quantities is lost entirely as deadweight loss. Option A reverses the actual output comparison, option B is too extreme since consumer surplus shrinks but is not necessarily zero, and option C is incorrect since producer surplus (profit) can actually rise under monopoly even as total surplus falls.

    Concept Tested: Deadweight Loss

    6. Under third-degree price discrimination, why does the group with less elastic demand get charged a higher price?

    1. Because less elastic demand means buyers are highly price-sensitive and easily switch away
    2. Because firms are legally required to charge less elastic groups more
    3. Because a less price-sensitive group will still buy nearly the same quantity even at a higher price, allowing the firm to raise price without losing much in sales
    4. Because less elastic groups always have lower willingness to pay
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. A group with less elastic demand responds weakly to price changes, so the firm can charge it more without losing many sales, maximizing profit by setting \(MR_1 = MR_2 = MC\) across the two markets. Option A describes elastic, not inelastic, demand, option B invents a legal requirement that does not exist, and option D incorrectly links elasticity to willingness to pay, which are related but distinct concepts.

    Concept Tested: Third-Degree Discrimination

    7. A monopolist faces demand \(P = 80 - 2Q\) and has constant marginal cost \(MC = 20\). What are the profit-maximizing quantity and price?

    1. \(Q=15\), \(P=\$50\)
    2. \(Q=20\), \(P=\$40\)
    3. \(Q=30\), \(P=\$20\)
    4. \(Q=10\), \(P=\$60\)
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Marginal revenue is \(MR = 80 - 4Q\); setting \(MR=MC\) gives \(80-4Q=20 \implies Q=15\), and the price from the demand curve is \(P = 80 - 2(15) = \$50\). The other options come from setting \(P=MC\) instead of \(MR=MC\), or from arithmetic slips in solving for \(Q\).

    Concept Tested: Monopoly Profit Maximization

    8. Demand is \(P = 150 - Q\) and marginal cost is constant at \(MC = 30\) with no fixed costs. What is the deadweight loss from monopoly in this market?

    1. $900
    2. $1,800
    3. $3,600
    4. $2,700
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. The competitive quantity is \(Q^C = 120\) (where \(P=MC\)), and the monopoly quantity is \(Q^M = 60\) (where \(MR = 150-2Q = 30\)), giving a monopoly price of \(P^M = \$90\). The deadweight loss triangle is \(\frac{1}{2}\times(90-30)\times(120-60) = \frac{1}{2}\times 60\times 60 = \$1{,}800\). The other options result from using only one of the two differences correctly or doubling the triangle's area instead of halving it.

    Concept Tested: Deadweight Loss

    9. A firm practices third-degree price discrimination between adults (\(P_1 = 60 - Q_1\)) and students (\(P_2 = 40 - Q_2\)), with constant marginal cost \(MC = 10\) in both markets. What are the profit-maximizing prices in each market?

    1. \(P_1 = \$35\), \(P_2 = \$25\)
    2. \(P_1 = \$25\), \(P_2 = \$35\)
    3. \(P_1 = \$30\), \(P_2 = \$20\)
    4. \(P_1 = \$45\), \(P_2 = \$15\)
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Setting \(MR_1 = 60-2Q_1 = 10\) gives \(Q_1=25\) and \(P_1 = 60-25=\$35\); setting \(MR_2 = 40-2Q_2=10\) gives \(Q_2=15\) and \(P_2=40-15=\$25\). Adults, whose demand curve reflects less elastic demand at comparable prices, correctly pay more, consistent with the third-degree discrimination rule; the other options either reverse the two prices or use incorrect quantities.

    Concept Tested: Third-Degree Discrimination

    10. A natural monopoly's efficient marginal-cost price lies below its average total cost, meaning marginal-cost pricing would force the firm into a loss equal to its fixed cost. Which regulatory approach allows the firm to remain financially viable without a subsidy, while still achieving more efficiency than an unregulated monopoly price?

    1. Marginal-cost pricing, since it is always the most efficient option regardless of financial viability
    2. Leaving the monopoly unregulated, since any form of regulation reduces efficiency
    3. Average-cost pricing, which sets price equal to average total cost so the firm breaks even while output remains closer to the efficient level than under unregulated monopoly
    4. Eliminating the natural monopoly entirely by forcing in multiple competing firms, regardless of cost duplication
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Average-cost pricing sets price equal to average total cost, letting the firm cover all its costs (zero economic profit) while keeping price well below the unregulated monopoly price and output closer to the efficient level, a practical middle ground. Option A ignores that marginal-cost pricing causes an unsustainable loss without a subsidy, option B ignores the substantial deadweight loss of unregulated monopoly, and option D ignores the cost-based rationale for natural monopoly, where duplicating infrastructure would raise, not lower, total cost.

    Concept Tested: Average-Cost Pricing Regulation
