    Quiz: Introduction to Microeconomics

    Test your understanding of scarcity, opportunity cost, marginal analysis, and the Production Possibilities Frontier with these review questions.

    1. What does the term "scarcity" mean in economics?

    1. A temporary shortage caused by a sudden spike in demand for a good
    2. The condition in which human wants for goods and services exceed the limited resources available to satisfy them
    3. A market outcome in which quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at the current price
    4. The gradual decline in the purchasing power of money over time
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Scarcity is the fundamental economic problem: because resources like time, labor, and land are limited while human wants are effectively unlimited, every individual and society must make choices about how to use them. Option A describes a temporary market shortage, option C describes a market surplus, and option D describes inflation — none of these is the basic definition of scarcity itself.

    Concept Tested: Scarcity

    2. Which of the following best defines "opportunity cost"?

    1. The total amount of money spent on a purchase, including taxes and fees
    2. A cost that has already been incurred and cannot be recovered
    3. The value of the next-best alternative given up when a choice is made
    4. The accounting profit a firm reports after subtracting explicit costs from revenue
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Opportunity cost is the value of the best forgone alternative, which is why it can include hidden costs like foregone wages, not just money actually spent. Option A describes explicit monetary cost, option B is actually the definition of a sunk cost (a common point of confusion), and option D describes accounting profit, a distinct concept from opportunity cost.

    Concept Tested: Opportunity Cost

    3. What distinguishes a "sunk cost" from other costs, and how should it be treated in decision-making?

    1. It is a cost that has already been paid and cannot be recovered, so it should not influence future decisions
    2. It is the best alternative forgone when a decision is made, so it should always be subtracted from total benefit
    3. It is a cost that varies directly with the level of output produced, so it should be minimized
    4. It is the additional cost of producing one more unit, so it should be compared to marginal benefit
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. A sunk cost, such as a non-refundable concert ticket already purchased, cannot be recovered no matter what choice is made next, so rational decision-making ignores it and focuses only on future costs and benefits. Option B describes opportunity cost, option C describes variable cost, and option D describes marginal cost — each a different concept from a sunk cost.

    Concept Tested: Sunk Cost

    4. According to the decision rule of marginal analysis, a rational decision-maker should continue an activity up to the point where:

    1. Total benefit is maximized regardless of total cost
    2. Average cost equals average benefit
    3. Marginal cost exceeds marginal benefit by the largest possible margin
    4. Marginal benefit equals marginal cost
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Marginal analysis compares the additional benefit of one more unit of an activity to its additional cost, and the optimal point is where marginal benefit (MB) equals marginal cost (MC) — pursuing the activity further would cost more than it is worth. Option A ignores costs entirely, option B confuses marginal with average measures, and option C describes the opposite of the optimal stopping point.

    Concept Tested: Marginal Analysis

    5. A student notices that each additional hour of studying raises her exam score by a smaller amount than the previous hour did. This pattern illustrates which principle?

    1. Increasing opportunity cost, since studying becomes more costly over time
    2. Diminishing marginal benefit, in which each additional unit of an activity provides less extra benefit than the one before
    3. The law of supply, since more studying requires supplying more effort
    4. Comparative advantage, since studying is the student's lowest-opportunity-cost activity
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. As shown in the chapter's study-hours example, the marginal benefit of each additional hour (grade points gained) falls as more hours are added — the first hour helps a lot, the fifth hour adds little. Option A misapplies a PPF concept to a single activity, option C misuses a supply-side concept, and option D is unrelated since only one activity (studying) is being considered.

    Concept Tested: Marginal Benefit

    6. On a Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF) diagram, a point located inside the curve represents:

    1. An inefficient outcome in which resources are idle or otherwise underused
    2. An efficient outcome in which all resources are fully employed
    3. A combination of goods that is currently unattainable given existing resources and technology
    4. The specific combination of goods that maximizes consumer satisfaction
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is A. Points inside the PPF are attainable but inefficient, meaning some resources are idle or wasted, such as during a recession. Option B describes points on the curve itself, option C describes points outside the curve, which are unattainable, and option D describes a consumer-choice concept not represented by the PPF, which shows production possibilities rather than preferences.

    Concept Tested: Production Frontier

    7. Why does the Production Possibilities Frontier typically bow outward instead of being drawn as a straight line?

    1. Because technology continuously improves while the PPF is being drawn
    2. Because the government imposes production quotas on each good
    3. Because resources are not equally well-suited to producing both goods, so each additional unit of one good requires giving up increasing amounts of the other
    4. Because consumer preferences shift between the two goods over time
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. The outward bow reflects the law of increasing opportunity cost: as production of one good expands, resources less suited to that good must be used, so each additional unit costs more of the other good than the one before. Option A describes a cause of PPF shifts rather than its shape, option B introduces an unrelated policy tool, and option D confuses production possibilities with consumer demand.

    Concept Tested: Increasing Opportunity Cost

    8. Which of the following statements is an example of normative economics?

    1. "Raising the price of gasoline reduces the quantity of gasoline consumed."
    2. "A country's unemployment rate fell by two percentage points last year."
    3. "Firms in perfectly competitive markets earn zero economic profit in the long run."
    4. "The government should lower income taxes because doing so is the fairer policy."
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is D. Normative statements are prescriptive, expressing what ought to happen based on values, and "should" combined with a fairness judgment cannot be settled with data alone. Options A, B, and C are all positive statements: descriptive, testable claims about what is true that could in principle be checked against evidence.

    Concept Tested: Normative Economics

    9. A graphic designer who earns $50 per hour at her regular job spends 3 unpaid hours designing invitations for her own wedding instead of hiring a designer who charges $20 per hour for the same work. What is the opportunity cost of designing the invitations herself?

    1. $20, the amount she would have paid a hired designer
    2. $150, the income she gave up by not working those 3 hours at her regular rate
    3. $0, because she did not pay anyone directly for the work
    4. $170, the sum of the hired designer's fee and her forgone wages
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is B. Her opportunity cost is the value of her best forgone alternative — working 3 hours at $50/hour, or $150 — not the cost of hiring someone else. Option A mistakes the price of an alternative service for her own opportunity cost, option C ignores the hidden cost of forgone wages entirely, and option D incorrectly combines two unrelated costs.

    Concept Tested: Opportunity Cost

    10. Country A can produce 1 ton of steel at an opportunity cost of 2 tons of wheat, while Country B can produce 1 ton of steel at an opportunity cost of 4 tons of wheat. Based on the principle of comparative advantage, which country should specialize in producing steel?

    1. Country B, because it has a lower opportunity cost of producing wheat
    2. Neither country, because comparative advantage only applies when opportunity costs are identical
    3. Country A, because it has the lower opportunity cost of producing steel
    4. Both countries equally, since total world output is unaffected by who produces what
    Show Answer

    The correct answer is C. Comparative advantage belongs to the producer with the lower opportunity cost of a good, regardless of absolute productivity; since Country A gives up only 2 tons of wheat per ton of steel versus Country B's 4 tons, Country A should specialize in steel. Option A correctly implies Country B should specialize in wheat but misstates why for steel, option B misunderstands that comparative advantage requires differing opportunity costs, and option D ignores that specialization based on comparative advantage increases total output.

    Concept Tested: Comparative Advantage
